# AuraWell — Lean Canvas

> This canvas captures a **working project hypothesis**, not validated market research. Validate each assumption with intended users, clinicians, and relevant privacy and safety stakeholders before treating it as a product plan.

## Problem

1. Young people may have difficulty finding approachable, timely ways to talk about everyday wellness concerns.
2. People may be unsure how to organize self-reported symptoms and basic measurements before seeking professional advice.
3. Early-stage wellness prototypes often separate conversational support, structured self-checks, and follow-up records across different experiences.

**Existing alternatives:** general-purpose chat tools, symptom-search websites, paper or spreadsheet check-ins, and direct consultation with qualified health professionals. These are not equivalent substitutes for clinical care.

## Customer segments

- **Primary users (hypothesis):** young adults and students seeking accessible wellness reflection and symptom organization.
- **Potential institutional users (hypothesis):** campus wellness programs, student support teams, and community health initiatives.
- **Early adopters (hypothesis):** digitally comfortable student groups and program coordinators willing to evaluate a non-diagnostic prototype.

## Unique value proposition

**A single, approachable workspace for wellness conversation, structured symptom reflection, and session-based check-in tracking—with clear limits that it is not a medical diagnosis.**

## Solution

- A broadly conversational AuraWell Assistant powered by a server-side Gemini API integration.
- A symptom-triage flow that uses current reported symptoms, age, vital measurements, duration, sleep, and optional notes.
- An input-based heuristic screening index with visible contributing factors.
- A health ledger for reviewing check-ins during the current session.

## Channels

- Direct demonstrations and usability sessions with student or campus wellness groups.
- Potential future collaboration with educational institutions and community health organizations.
- Project showcase materials and the application website.

These are proposed discovery channels, not current partnerships or confirmed distribution.

## Revenue streams

No revenue model is implemented or validated. Possible future models to investigate include institutional licensing or grant-supported deployment. Any commercial model would require appropriate clinical, privacy, legal, and operational review.

## Cost structure

- Gemini API usage and production hosting.
- Engineering, testing, accessibility, and ongoing maintenance.
- Clinical safety review, privacy and security assessment, and any required compliance work.
- User research and institutional onboarding if the project advances beyond a prototype.

## Key metrics

- Task completion and abandonment rates for chat, triage, and ledger flows.
- User comprehension of the non-diagnostic limitations and score explanation.
- Whether triage summaries accurately reflect the submitted inputs.
- Rate of API failures, invalid AI responses, and successful recovery.
- Usability and accessibility findings across mobile and desktop.

Metrics should be evaluated without collecting unnecessary personal or identifiable health data.

## Unfair advantage

No defensible unfair advantage has been established. Potential assets to test—not claims—include a focused SDG 3 framing, an integrated prototype workflow, and the ability to iterate with student and campus wellness feedback.

## Assumptions to validate

1. Intended users want these three workflows in one application.
2. Users understand that generated assessments are not diagnoses.
3. The score factor breakdown improves understanding without creating false confidence.
4. Appropriate organizations are willing to support a prototype only after adequate safety and privacy review.

## Non-goals of the current prototype

- Replacing clinicians, emergency services, or professional mental-health support.
- Providing a validated clinical risk model or disease diagnosis.
- Maintaining a secure longitudinal medical record or user account.
- Claiming institutional partnerships, clinical effectiveness, or a proven business model.
